# Product context

Carried forward from `Fittr MVP Requirements.md` for downstream skills (PRD, UX, Phase 2 planning) that need the full-MVP background behind Phase 1's narrower scope.

## Problems Fittr addresses

1. People forget what they own — their wardrobe is physically available but there's no convenient way to browse it digitally.
2. Choosing outfits is repetitive — "what goes with these pants," "have I worn this before" get asked over and over with no aid.
3. Fashion inspiration is disconnected from the user's own wardrobe (deferred to Phase 2's social/discover layer).
4. Wardrobe organization alone can feel like inventory management — the app has to be useful, not just a database.
5. Users have no lightweight place to share outfits without a general-purpose social network (deferred to Phase 2).

## Target users

Consumers who already own enough clothing to benefit from digital organization, care at least somewhat about personal style, regularly think about what to wear, and use their phone for fashion inspiration. Not every fashion consumer — the product optimizes for people already motivated to organize and experiment with their wardrobe.

## Jobs to be done

- Primary: "When deciding what to wear, I want to see and combine the clothes I own so I can put together an outfit quickly."
- Secondary: save an outfit to wear again; save an outfit as inspiration; plan outfits ahead of time; share an outfit once proud of it; discover ideas from others' outfits (the last two are Phase 2).

## Product principle

Fittr should not launch as "Instagram for fashion + AI + digital closet + shopping." It should launch as "your digital wardrobe for creating, planning, and sharing Fits." The MVP's job — which Phase 1 carries alone — is to prove the wardrobe/outfit loop provides enough recurring value to support a larger fashion platform later. AI is meant to remove friction (background removal), not become the core experience; Phase 1 keeps category detection manual for the same reason.
